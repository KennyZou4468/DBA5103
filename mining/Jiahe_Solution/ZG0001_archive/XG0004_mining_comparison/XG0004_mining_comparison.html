All three Year 1 optima buy Copper, buy Steel, and farm Zone 2. Resource budgets change when that strategy becomes affordable and how much it earns.

  • Full resources: earliest target Day 75; 177,440.85g at Day 112.
  • 90 to 134 energy: year-end cash rises from 26,946.02g to 80,467.45g.
  • Copper and Steel pay back; buying Gold immediately reduces cash.

02 · Energy and progression

Clearing one floor unlocks faster growth

The minimum for the first floor is 132 energy for ordinary rocks, or 133.65776 including the expected gems collected by the compact model. A whole-number budget of 134 removes the early progression barrier.

The first floor requires 33 ordinary rocks. With Basic, each rock needs two swings and every swing costs two energy: the rocks alone therefore need 132 energy. Expected gems add 1.65776 energy. The model can clear a first floor at 133.65776, but not at 133.65766; 134 is the smallest whole-number budget covering the full bundle.

At 90 energy, the Basic pickaxe cannot clear a floor. The player repeatedly harvests a partial first floor until Copper is affordable on Day 36; Zone 2 is delayed until Day 76. At 134 energy, Basic clears one floor per day, Copper arrives on Day 27 and Zone 2 opens on Day 37. After Steel on Day 43, the plan farms Zone 2 from Day 45 for about 1,180.51g per day.

Time remains slack: peak daily mining time is 263.38 of 900 minutes in Case A, 92.19 of 300 in Case B, and 138.99 of 300 in Case C. The added energy accelerates progression and cash generation; more time alone would not remove the initial rock-clearance barrier.

03 · Strategy and validation

Upgrade promptly when it pays back

The strategy in one sentence
With enough time to recover the investment, buy Copper and Steel as soon as affordable, unlock and repeatedly farm Zone 2, and skip Gold unless deeper progression is required.

In all three Year 1 plans, Copper and Steel are submitted on their first affordable morning. Delaying one purchase by a day lowers cash after reoptimization; these tests do not prove that every later date is worse.

Delay tests fix only the delayed tier. Gold tests use its first affordable baseline date and retain baseline Copper/Steel dates. Other actions are reoptimized; positive values are cash losses.

Gold lowers cash when forced on its first affordable morning. The short-horizon exception also matters: the full-resource Day-28 optimum skips Steel, and both constrained Day-28 plans buy no upgrades.

Strict full clearance deadlocks Case B at 166.67g. Case C stays feasible: Copper on Day 27, Steel on Day 48, and 53,391.54g at Day 112. One-day submission lockout also preserves Copper, Steel and Zone 2 in all cases. These are tested patterns, not a universal theorem.

Target-day proof and scope

Full-resource optimal bounds are 99,494.50g at Day 74 and 101,545.72g at Day 75. Feasible plans can be extended by idle days, proving Day 75 is earliest. Both constrained Day-112 optima are below target, proving it unreachable within Year 1; later dates were not solved.

Verification: Gurobi optimality plus independent reconstruction of unrounded cash, resources, upgrades and zone access. Results retain the compact solver’s assumption that all generated gems and containers on an entered floor are collected. Expected yields are not real-game guarantees.

Audit note: compact cross-zone ordering has an open constraint gap (page 4). Reported optima solve the current coded model; full equivalence to the intended route rules is not established.

CSV columns describe a solved plan. Treating outputs as fresh decisions or inventory can double-count cash, material and state during integration.
